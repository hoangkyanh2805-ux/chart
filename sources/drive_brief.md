PHẦN 1 — HOOK (87 từ)
A viewer wrote me this: "I was a college dropout, down to my last bit of savings, and decided to teach myself trading from scratch." He watched all two hundred and thirty videos. But here is what most people miss. The stop loss came first. The entry came second. That order is the whole video.

PHẦN 2 — PROBLEM (158 từ)
Let me tell you what usually happens instead. A new trader opens a chart. Finds a setup. Thinks about where to enter. Where to take profit. How much to make. And then — maybe, if they remember — thinks about the stop loss. Last. As an afterthought. In the nine hundred and eighty-five comments I work from, entry comes up ninety times. Stop loss comes up twenty times. That ratio is the problem. People are asking about entry four times more than they are asking about risk. And then they wonder why the account is gone. The college dropout had it backwards too, at first. He said he was down to his last savings. That is what learning entry-first does. It teaches you to chase the win before you have learned to protect what you have. The result is always the same: a small account, a big loss, and a lesson that cost more than it should have.

PHẦN 3 — ROOT CAUSE (176 từ)
So why does everyone learn it backwards? Two reasons. Reason one: the education system is built around entry. Every course, every video, every mentor leads with the setup. Where to enter. What indicator to confirm. What pattern to watch. Risk management is the chapter at the end — the one nobody finishes. In the nine hundred and eighty-five comments, education gap is the biggest cluster at four hundred and fifty-four. Risk management is one hundred and thirty-four. People are asking for the setup because that is what they have been taught to ask for. Reason two: defining the loss feels like admitting you might be wrong. New traders think a stop loss is a confession. It is not. It is a budget. It is the cost of being wrong, decided in advance, while you are calm. The college dropout learned this the hard way — down to his last savings. But he learned it. And that is why his stop loss now matches a teacher's salary. Not because he is smart. Because he learned the order.

PHẦN 4 — FRAMEWORK 3 BƯỚC (393 từ)
The order is three steps. Define the loss first. Then the entry. Then the size. Let me take you through them. Step one: define the loss before you open the chart. Before you look at a single candle. You decide: if I am wrong about this trade, I am willing to lose this much. Write it down. In dollars, in R, in whatever unit you use. But write it down before you see the setup. Because once you see the setup, your brain will negotiate. It will move the stop loss. It will make it wider. It will say "just this once." That is not analysis. That is emotion wearing an analysis costume. Step two: find the entry that respects the loss. Not the other way around. Most people find an entry and then try to fit a stop loss around it. That is backwards. You have a fixed loss budget. Now you look for an entry where that budget makes sense. Where the stop loss sits at a level that is actually defensible — not so tight that normal noise takes you out, not so wide that the loss is unacceptable. The entry serves the loss. Not the other way around. Step three: size the position so the loss is exactly what you budgeted. This is where most people get confused. They think size means "how much can I make." No. Size means "how much am I willing to lose, and what position size makes that loss equal to my budget." If your budget is fifty dollars and your stop loss is fifty pips away, your size is one dollar per pip. If your budget is fifty dollars and your stop loss is ten pips away, your size is five dollars per pip. The loss is always fifty dollars. The size changes. The loss does not. That is the whole system. Define the loss. Find the entry that respects it. Size to the loss. Three steps. In that order. Always. And notice what this does to the psychology. When the loss is defined first, the entry becomes a search problem, not a hope problem. You are not hoping the trade works. You are looking for a place where your pre-defined loss makes sense. That is a completely different mental state. It is calm. It is patient. It is the opposite of chasing.

PHẦN 5 — EXAMPLE (236 từ)
Let me make this concrete. And I will label it clearly: this is an educational illustration of the mechanics — not a trade recommendation, not a signal. Picture XAUUSD. You have been watching the higher timeframe. Price has been making higher highs and higher lows. Your bias is long. You have one level you are watching — a previous high that you expect to act as support. That is the plan. Now, before you do anything else, you define the loss. You say: I am willing to lose thirty dollars on this trade. That is written down. Now you look for the entry. You drop to the lower timeframe. Price pulls back to your level. It shows a rejection candle. That is your entry signal. You place the stop loss below the swing low — twenty pips away. Your size: thirty dollars divided by twenty pips, so one dollar and fifty cents per pip. Total risk: thirty dollars. Exactly as budgeted. Now the hard part. Price enters. Moves against you. Hits the stop. You lose thirty dollars. That is it. That is the whole loss. Not thirty dollars plus revenge trading. Not thirty dollars plus "I will just hold it." Thirty dollars. Done. The next day, you do it again. And the next. That is how the college dropout built his capital. Not by being right every time. By being wrong cheaply, consistently, and without emotion.

PHẦN 6 — CHECKLIST (165 từ)
Write these five lines down and run them before every trade. One: what is my loss budget for this trade, written down before I saw the setup? Two: where is my stop loss, and is it at a defensible level — not too tight, not too wide? Three: does my entry respect my stop loss, or am I moving the stop loss to fit the entry? Four: what is my position size, and does it make my total loss equal to my budget? Five: if I am wrong, is the loss exactly what I decided I could handle? That is it. Five lines. And here is your drill for the next seven days. Every time you see a setup, write down the loss budget first. Then check if the entry respects it. Do not take the trade. Just write it down. Seven days of that trains the order more than a month of live trading will — and it costs you nothing but a notebook.
